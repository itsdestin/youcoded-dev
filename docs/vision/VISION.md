# What YouCoded is for

## In one sentence

YouCoded is the start of a new kind of personal computing: one open app through which
anyone, with no software skill at all, can make every device they own work for them —
build their own apps, run their own home, hold their own data, and help build the app itself.

## What the name means

Mostly the Gen Z slang: **"that's so you-coded"**, meaning unmistakably *you*. Your technology,
shaped until it reads as yours. The second reading, "you coded it" (a non-developer built this by
asking AI; a nod to Claude Code), is true but secondary. Destin's words: section 5 of the voice
notes.

## Today versus where it is going

**Today** it is mostly an AI assistant: a chat window, on desktop, Android and the web, that
can do real work on your computer with any AI model you choose, plus Themes, Pages, skills,
an Office editor, a browser and a community marketplace.

**That is the doorway, not the destination.** The destination is an *everything app*: the home
base for your whole digital life — documents, money, home, devices, social life, automations —
on technology you own, can change, and never have to rent.

Do not judge YouCoded by what it is today. Judge today's work by how far it moves toward this.

## The premise: everyone who downloads it becomes a developer

Ordinary open-source software lets you contribute *if you want to*, and almost nobody does,
because contributing takes programming skill. YouCoded's premise is different: **every person
who installs it can change it**, simply by describing what they want. A new look, a new
page, a fix for something annoying, a whole new app inside the app — and then share it with
everyone else through the marketplace.

That makes YouCoded meant to be the world's first truly *crowdsourced* application: not built
by a company or by a few volunteer programmers, but by its users, all of them. Destin is the
first proof: a non-programmer who directed the whole app into existence, replaced Google,
Microsoft and paid services in his own life with it, and built things (like a Linux swipe
keyboard) that had sat unsolved for years.

## Why it matters

For twenty-plus years technology has moved toward "own nothing and be happy": everything a
subscription, every program licensed rather than owned, every person's data held by a few
companies. Those companies kept their power partly because they could hire nearly every
capable engineer and outbuild anyone who tried to compete.

AI breaks that. When anyone can direct an AI to build software, engineering talent stops being
something only the richest companies can buy. Anything a private company can make, a
passionate open community can now make better. YouCoded is one channel for turning that shift
into real freedom for ordinary people — and, at its largest, part of an economic change in who
holds power over software and data.

## What it has to become (the pillars)

1. **Yours.** It looks, behaves and works the way *you* want. Any AI model, never locked to one
   company — including free models that run on your own device with no internet at all. Your
   data and your agents are not held by anyone else.
2. **Built by describing.** Themes, Pages, skills, agents and automations are made by saying what
   you want. Any open-source app can be pulled in and made to fit. No code required, ever.
3. **Everywhere, as one (the Mesh).** Desktop, phone, web — and eventually every device you own —
   act as one system: your work follows you, runs on whichever device is on, and is backed up
   across all of them, without you managing any of it.
4. **Your own private cloud.** You host your own data. Real privacy, not a policy promise.
5. **Replaces, not just assists.** Office suites, smart-home apps, finance apps, social feeds:
   open tools inside YouCoded take the place of paid, closed services.
6. **Built together.** Every user can improve the app and share what they made. The marketplace
   is "GitHub for normal people": publish, fork, customise, review.
7. **For everyone.** Students, professionals, everyday people — not just developers. Designed by a
   non-developer for non-developers; if a step needs technical knowledge, that step is a bug.

## How to use this when making decisions

- **Ask "does this let more people shape their own technology?"** Work that turns something only
  Destin or a developer could do into something any user can do is core, not a side feature.
- **Breadth is the point; trust is the price of admission.** An "everything app" is supposed to be
  broad. But no one will build their life on it unless the basics — chat, saving, sync, speed —
  feel rock-solid. Reliability work serves the vision; it does not compete with it.
- **Every technical hurdle is a wall.** Needing a GitHub account, a terminal, a networking tool or
  an API key is a place where the "anyone" in the premise fails. Count these as real problems.
- **No new lock-in.** Prefer open formats, local options and the user's own storage over anything
  that ties people to YouCoded, a cloud, or one AI company.
- **Think in building blocks.** A feature others can reuse, remix and share (a page, an agent, a
  skill) multiplies; a one-off feature does not.
