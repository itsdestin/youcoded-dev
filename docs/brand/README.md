# YouCoded brand guide

Read this before making anything the public will see: the website, a film, a poster, an ad, a
post, a listing, a README, release notes. `docs/brand/guide.html` shows it all drawn.

- **Not covered:** the app's own screens (the theme paints them; see
  `docs/active/design/2026-10-13-ui-design-guide.md`). The first-run setup screens are the one place
  the app uses the brand look.
- **History:** logo and icons from the Oct 2026 rounds
  (`docs/archive/design/2026-10-01-brand-identity-v2/DECISIONS.md`). Everything else here is current.
- **Changes** a reader would notice need Destin's approval. Fix a wrong value or path and tell him.

---

## 1. The brand

youcoded is one open app through which anyone, with no software skill, makes their technology
work for them. You make youcoded do what you want by telling it: it becomes your own app and your
own assistant, shaped to how you live. Share what you made, and everyone who installs youcoded can
use it and build on it. Today it is mostly an AI assistant; that is the doorway. The destination is
an everything app on technology you own. Built entirely through conversation with AI by someone
who has never written code. Full vision: `docs/vision/VISION.md`.

**Stands for:** software you own, not rent. **Written for:** students, professionals and everyday
AI users. Developers are welcome but are not who we write for.

## 2. Name and lines

**Lowercase by default** (Destin, 2026-11-18). If it would look normal on a poster, sticker or
social post, write **youcoded** and keep the whole line lowercase (acronyms like "AI" keep their
capitals). If it would look normal in a letter, legal page, press release, store description or
full paragraph, write **YouCoded** in normally capitalised sentences, and never start a sentence
with lowercase "youcoded".

| | |
|---|---|
| **Tagline** | open source everything |
| **One-line description** | your personal AI assistant and open source everything app (formal: "YouCoded is your personal AI assistant and open source everything app.") |
| **Headline** | make your technology useful, fun, yours. |
| **Web address** | **youcoded.ai**, always all lowercase: never capitals, small caps or "www.". On its own, Outfit 600; never JetBrains Mono. |
| **Initials** | yc, only as the initials tile |

**Never:** Youcoded, You Coded, YOUCODED, YC. **Retired:** "agents for everyone", "YouCoded
Assistant", "a GUI for Claude Code".
**Related:** say "the youcoded marketplace" and "community themes", not "WeCoded" (that's only a
GitHub folder name). "Pages" with a capital P means the feature. The company, Destin's Adventures,
LLC, appears only in legal text.

## 3. Logo

Typed, not drawn. There is no logo image file. **The live website is the reference**
(`youcoded/docs/index.html`, the "THE LOGO" styles): copy from it, never from older marketing.

- **Recipe:** Outfit 600, letter spacing −3.5%. "you" in a 100° gradient `#9D5BD0` → `#D25AA0`;
  "coded" in plum `#21152C`, or `#F3EAF9` on dark.
- **Versions:** icon + name + tagline (main) · icon + name (footer, end cards) · stacked name over
  tagline, no icon (setup screens) · name only · "yc" tile (approved, unused).
- **Clear space:** half the icon's height on every side. **Minimums:** icon 16px, lockup 96px wide,
  name 64px wide.
- **Other themes:** the name takes that theme's colours, only in pieces about themes (the website's
  `BRAND` table has them). Everything else uses the purple version.
- **Don't:** use another font or weight, capitals, a different gradient or one on "coded", outlines,
  glows, 3D, stretching, a busy background, or the mascot in place of a letter.

- **Mostly lavender and plum, a little purple:** roughly 70% background, 20% text, 10% purple. One
  purple word per headline.
- **Never pure black or grey.** At most one extra colour per campaign, and the purple must still lead.
- **Readable:** purple text on lavender and white on purple buttons. On lilac panels, purple only for
  big or bold words. The gradient and the tagline grey are for large type only.
- **Not our look:** purple-to-blue gradients, glowing orbs, sparkles, particles, circuit lines.

## 7. Layout

1. **One spacing scale:** every gap is 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 or 8 × the base unit (8px on the
   web; short side ÷ 60 in video and posters).
2. **Group, then space the groups:** the gap between groups is at least twice the gap inside one.
3. **Safe margins:** nothing within 8% of the edge (in vertical video, also clear the top 12% and
   bottom 20%). Too long? Shrink or wrap, never touch the edge.
4. **One alignment per piece:** all centred or all left-aligned. Centred blocks sit slightly above
   the middle.
5. **Three levels at most:** what to remember (logo or headline), what to do (web address, button),
   fine print. Each clearly smaller and softer than the one above.

**End card** (base unit *b*): icon 14*b* · gap 2*b* · logo 7*b* · gap 6*b* · `youcoded.ai` 3*b*
(Outfit 600, plum) · gap 1*b* · "Free · Windows · Mac · Linux · Android" 2*b* (DM Sans 500,
secondary text). Centred, on lavender, held at least 2.5 s.

## 9. Voice

**Hopeful, clear, honest, a little defiant.** Never doom-y, corporate, smug, or insulting to people
who use Apple, Google or Microsoft.

**Always assume the audience** is smart, informed and quick to learn, with little time and little
interest in mundane features or tech specs. So:
- Never talk down: no over-explaining, no stacked rhetorical tricks ("imagine a world…").
- Don't simplify until the meaning is lost.
- Technical terms only where they help, explained the first time ("your own computer", not "local
  inference").
- Say each thing once per page, film or campaign.

**Edit every draft twice.** First pass, ask: *"How can I cut 20% of this without changing the
meaning, while making it more meaningful, useful and impactful?"* Final pass, ask: *"Is this
concise and punchy? Does every word, frame and page earn its value?"* Anything that doesn't, goes.

**Never undersell, never blend in.** Sell the vision, not a soulless feature list. Don't make the pitch
about what the rivals *in that piece's comparison* also offer (section 10). **Test:** nobody should finish a piece asking "so… why
not just use Claude Code or Hermes?"

- **Show one concrete example** over a list of adjectives, written to the rules below.

### The pitch: you are building your own youcoded (Destin, 2026-11-18)

"The idea is that you are customizing youcoded. You are building youcoded. You are making your own
app, your own assistant that does whatever … you want it to do. And everything else is just
evidence towards that end."

- **Say "make youcoded do X", never "build a page for X".** The audience has never used youcoded
  and does not know what a "page" is. Do not use "page" or "Pages" for a thing youcoded makes in
  anything aimed at newcomers.
- **An example is evidence, never a feature.** Nothing in an example may read as something that
  comes in the download. The smart-home and money dashboards are **not included in the app**: they
  are things Destin made for himself. Never present them, or anything like them, as features.
- **Every example stands on its own.** It must carry what the person wants, why it is worth
  wanting, and how it differs from what already exists. "Build a home page" is neither cool nor
  unique: Google Home and Apple Home already do that. The difference is that it is made to *your*
  wishes. His example of the right shape: "I want youcoded to help me manage my smart home. I
  don't care about TVs, I already have remotes, but I would like my lights, my light controls and
  my thermostat settings on the front page. Just limit it to my room, put all the other rooms on a
  different page or further down."
- **Stop reaching for the home and the money.** Those two were used in nearly every marketing
  piece up to 2026-11-18. Think more broadly: what would this particular viewer want their
  technology to do that nothing they own will do for them? Use the home or money example only
  when it is the best one for that piece, never by default, and never both.
- **Test:** could the example be answered with "there is already an app for that"? Then it has
  not shown the customising, and it is not ready.
- **Requests shown on screen sound like a real person** (Destin, 2026-11-19: "this isn't how
  any real person would ask for this... the fake prompts have honestly been one of the greatest
  weaknesses of our marketing so far"). Lower case, unsure, a few messages back and forth with
  the app answering, never one polished command: "can i change how this looks?", a reply, then
  "idk. kinda rainy tokyo type vibe? open to ideas". Word them so someone who has never used
  the app understands: no names for parts of the app.
- **Invite, never report.** Never credit the viewer with something they have not done ("you
  asked for it", "you made this"). Destin: "everything here exists with *potential* but has not
  happened... we should implore rather than state when addressing users directly." Good: "make
  anything you can imagine, just by asking", "stop waiting for updates".
- **No airy slogans.** A line must say something the reader can picture or check. Destin on
  "nobody can lock you out", "ask for it" and the like: "airy and lack meaning".
- **Respect developers:** "you don't need to code", never "coders are obsolete".
- **Destin's story** (no coding background; dropped Microsoft, Apple and Google) is our strongest
  material. Quote him from `docs/vision/2026-10-22-why-youcoded-voice-notes.md`; never paraphrase.
  Posts under his name use his own looser voice (`destins-writing-style` skill).
- **Banned (Destin):** real app, real files, actually reads, does real work, self-improving.
  **Clichés:** unleash, supercharge, seamless, revolutionize, next-generation, game-changer, AI
  copilot, the future of…. **Jargon:** agentic, harness, LLM, model-agnostic, BYOK, artifacts.

## 10. Claims

Every claim must be true in today's download. Check the app's source, not the August fact sheet.

**Lead with what sets youcoded apart:**
- **It becomes what you ask for:** tell it what you want it to do and it reshapes itself: new
  abilities, small apps of your own inside it, whole new looks, without code.
- **Made for people who don't code.** Rival agents were built by and for developers.
- **Everyone can build it:** themes, skills and more are shared through the marketplace.
- **One home for your digital life:** chat, files, an Office editor, a browser, games with
  friends, on every device. The Android app runs the assistant on the phone itself.

**Table stakes against open rivals** (Hermes, OpenCode and the like have them; when a piece
compares youcoded with the broader market, state these briefly, never as the pitch): any AI model
including free local ones · free and open source (MIT) · asks before acting · terminal and Claude
Code support.

**Judge uniqueness in the context of the piece** (Destin, 2026-11-19: "all ads/marketing should
consider broadly whether the feature is unique in the context its described"). The same fact can
be table stakes in one piece and the whole pitch in another. Against the closed assistants (the
Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini apps), "any AI model" is something they do not offer, so a piece aimed
at their users may lead with it. Before leading with anything, ask: who is this piece measuring
youcoded against, and do *they* have it?

**Never claim:** an iPhone app (iPhones use it in the browser, through your computer), a big
community (most add-ons are imported), developer-editor features, or that a smart-home or money
dashboard comes with the app (neither is included; section 9, The pitch). **Check first:** background
tasks, PDFs, automations, models running inside the Android app. **Numbers:** dated and sourced
only (2026-11-18: 302 marketplace add-ons, 13 made for youcoded).

**Competitors:** name them if useful; no comparison tables, and compare only on what sets youcoded
apart. Wherever Anthropic, OpenAI or OpenRouter are named at length, add: *"YouCoded is an
independent, community-built project. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or officially supported by
Anthropic, OpenAI, or OpenRouter."*
