# Session context panel — UX review 1 (context-free beta tester)

Tested 2026-09-09 against the workbench (simulated backend), midnight + light themes, 1440x900 and 390x844.
Screenshots live in `docs/archive/reviews/2026-09-09-session-context-panel-ux-review-1-shots/`.
Not tested: touch input and a 1.5x high-density screen — the capture tool cannot do either, so any
finding about tap targets or hover below is reasoned from size/behaviour, not observed on a touchscreen.

## Could I complete the task?

Mostly. In the two standalone panels I could answer "what was the assistant given, and what got cut"
in under a minute: the five tabs are well organised, the Overview's "What was left out" list is the
best thing here, and clicking a row on it jumps to the right tab, which felt smart. What I could not
do was read the cut material itself — "What it got" stops at a "…" with no way to continue (U2), and
the moment I pressed "Expand (44 lines)" the panel grew past the bottom of the window with nothing to
scroll and no way back (U1). That was also the single most confusing moment: I had pressed the one
control that promised more, and the result was a screen I could not move, could not finish reading,
and could only escape by closing the whole panel. A close second was discovering that the panel which
opens over my actual chat is a different panel — different title, no tabs, and much heavier jargon
("MCP servers dropped to fit the tools budget") — than the one I had just learned to use, so nothing I
learned in one carried over to the other.
