P-11 — Grey helper text becomes readable measured

The problem

The app has five levels of text colour, from strongest (fg, body text) to faintest (fg-faint). The second-faintest one, fg-muted, is used for the small helper lines you actually read: the sub-line under every Settings row, timestamps, file sizes, the status bar chips, "no results" copy. It sits mostly on raised surfaces (cards, rows, dialogs), and there it measured 3.0–3.3 : 1 in every built-in theme. The accessibility standard for small text is 4.5 : 1 (WCAG AA). 3 : 1 is the bar for large headlines, and the app's own theme checker was only demanding that.

The faintest level, fg-faint, is at about 2 : 1 — fine for a divider line or a disabled chevron, unreadable for a word. 18 places were using it for words anyway (the "Skip setup" link in the onboarding wizard, a timestamp, an "or", the placeholder in dropdowns…), and the loading spinner cycled through it as one of its colours.

What changed

  • In each of the four built-in themes, fg-muted was moved toward the body-text colour until it clears 4.5 : 1 on the raised surfaces. The step above it (fg-dim) — and in Crème the step above that — was nudged the same way so the three greys stay in order rather than collapsing into one.
  • fg-faint is now decorative-only: the 18 text uses moved up to fg-muted; dividers, dots, icon-only buttons and disabled chips keep it (60 places, each checked by hand). The spinner drops it from its cycle.
  • A test now pins all of this (fg-muted ≥ 4.5 : 1 on raised surfaces, ladder stays ordered, fg-faint stays decorative), so a future theme edit can't quietly regress it.

P-12 — Give the dark themes a real accent (A), or keep grey and add a second signal (B) judgment

The problem

Every theme has one "accent" colour that means this is the main action or this is the one that's selected: the primary button, the chosen model, the active theme card, the send arrow, your own chat bubble. In Midnight and Dark the accent is a light grey that is almost the same colour as body text (they differ by only 1.27 : 1 and 1.12 : 1). A light-grey block with dark text is also what a disabled control often looks like in other apps — so primary and selected things could read as switched off. Look at the Model & Effort picker below: "Max" is disabled and "Auto" is selected. Nothing measurable fails here — the contrast on those buttons is 9–12 : 1; the baseline distinguishes them by fill vs. dimming. This is a convention argument.

Light and Crème don't have this problem: their accent is near-black, and a black fill on a light screen is unmistakably "on". Halftone and Meadow bring their own accents (pink, green). So this only concerns the two dark built-ins.

Option A — a real accent colour

  • Midnight: #6CA0DC, a soft blue (it sits next to Midnight's blue links, which is deliberate — GitHub does the same).
  • Dark: #8DA6C9, a steel blue-grey, so Dark stays the quieter of the two.
  • Text on the accent stays dark (the canvas colour), as it is today. White text was considered and rejected by arithmetic: a blue dark enough for white text to reach 4.5 : 1 is too dark for blue text to be readable on the black background — and the app uses the accent as text in 47 places. Measured: dark-on-accent 6.9 : 1 (Midnight) and 7.6 : 1 (Dark); accent as text on a card 5.6 and 6.4 : 1.

Option B — keep the grey, add a second signal

  • The theme engine notices when a dark theme's accent is "just another grey" (within 1.5 : 1 of the text colour) and flags it. Only then, primary/selected controls get bold text plus a thin dark line just inside their edge (a double-border look), and disabled primary buttons lose their fill and take a dashed outline. Nothing changes in themes that have a real accent.
  • Side effect you can see below: your own chat bubble shares the primary-button recipe, so it gets the inner line too.
  • Not visible in any capture: the dashed "disabled" outline — no screen in the sweep has a disabled primary button.

P-13 — Your bubble on light themes: grey instead of solid black judgment

The problem

On Light and Crème the accent is near-black, so your own messages render as solid black blocks — the heaviest object on an otherwise pale screen. Nothing fails a number; this is a weight/taste argument.

What changed

The theme engine stamps the page with whether the current theme is a light or dark scheme. On light schemes the user bubble is drawn on the "inset" grey (the same surface as the assistant's bubble) with normal body text. Dark schemes keep their accent-coloured bubble. This is how claude.ai draws the user bubble in both modes.

What you will notice, and the risks

  • Your messages stop being the darkest thing on a light screen.
  • Scope: the rule keys on "light scheme", so every light community pack (Meadow Mist today) gets the grey bubble too. Halftone is dark and keeps its pink-glow bubble.

P-16 — Theme-pack fixes: outlines and glass in Meadow Mist and Halftone mixed

The problem

Two bundled community packs draw their panels as translucent glass over a wallpaper. In Meadow Mist the glass was 58 % solid, and its border colour is used at 50 % opacity on top of that — so outlined ("secondary") buttons and the edges of rows faded into the sky behind them (measured: finding T-5/T-6, the outline composited over the wallpaper bottoms out near 1 : 1). Halftone's glass was 78 %, readable — the Halftone part and the glass changes are judgment.

What changed (the visible half — the packs)

  • Meadow Mist: border-dim opacity 50 % → 80 %; panel glass 58 % → 85 % solid.
  • Halftone Dimension: panel glass 78 % → 85 % solid. Its checkboxes were already square, so nothing to do there.
  • These are edits to the copies bundled in the workbench for review; on approval the same edits go to the wecoded-themes repo.

The invisible half (only after approval)

The rules the theme checker enforces on every submitted pack would gain: border-dim on panel ≥ 1.5 : 1 (was 1.3), glass that carries text ≥ 85 % solid. The checker file lives in three repos and must be merged in a fixed order, which is why it isn't in this branch.
