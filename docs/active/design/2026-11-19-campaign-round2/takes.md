# Campaign round two: the words (second draft)

Rewritten from your 14 comments on the first draft. Still words only: nothing is recorded,
drawn or scored.

What your comments changed for every take:

- **Typed requests sound like a real person.** Lower case, unsure, a few messages back and
  forth with the app answering, never one polished command. And worded so someone who has
  never used the app understands them: no names for parts of the app.
- **Lines to the viewer invite, they never report.** Nothing says "you asked" or "you made":
  the viewer hasn't yet. Lines say what they could do.
- **No airy slogans.** A line has to say something concrete or it goes.
- **"Open source everything" only where the film has earned it.** That is take 1. The rest
  end on the youcoded name alone.
- **No crowds of strangers each making their own thing.** One person refining one thing, or
  two friends passing something back and forth. Take 5 is the exception you allowed.

---

## Take 1: "Once upon a time"

**For:** people who already feel something is wrong with big tech but have never heard there
is an alternative. The long YouTube and TV slot.

**What it is:** your script, told as a fairytale by a quick, mischievous British storybook
voice. One version only, the one that names their motives.

**What you see:** a storybook drawn in our purple and plum. A few tall lit towers over a town
where every window looks the same. When "something wonderful" happens, a purple light slips
out of a tower and drifts down into the town. Window by window, each one turns into a
different youcoded. The town ends up brighter than the towers.

> For more than a decade, a small handful of corporations have dictated how you interact
> with your technology.
>
> They decided what your computers could do, what it would cost, and what happened with
> your personal data.
>
> And we all... we all just accepted it. We accepted that we would subscribe to everything
> and own nothing.
>
> However, recently... something truly wonderful happened.
>
> Blinded by their own hubris, these corporations created a new kind of technology:
> artificial intelligence. A technology they believed would bring about a great treasure:
> shareholder value.
>
> But in their excitement, they created something far beyond their own control: a tool
> that can make software, the ever-scarce foundation of these monopolies, for anyone who
> asks.
>
> And so, for the first time in a very long while, we get to decide. What our computers
> can do. What it costs. And what happens with our data.
>
> It's time to free ourselves from the tech monopolies of the past.
>
> It's time, at long, long last... to open source everything.

**What changed:** your shareholder line and "at long, long last" are in as you wrote them.
"Technology" became "tool" where you marked it. The softer version B is gone. The bridge you
said didn't fit is replaced by one that hands back the same three things the corporations
took in your second paragraph, in the same storybook "we".

## Take 2: "Make it mine"

**For:** people who already use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini every day and have never imagined
they could change the app itself.

**What it is:** no narrator. One person, three short conversations with their youcoded, and
it changing in front of them. The same person throughout, refining as they go.

**A look**

> **them:** can i change how this whole thing looks?
>
> **youcoded:** Anything you like. What are you picturing?
>
> **them:** idk. kinda rainy tokyo at night type vibe? open to ideas

The whole app turns to neon and rain.

> **them:** ok yeah. less pink tho

It shifts.

**Their home**

> **them:** i want youcoded to manage my smart home
>
> **youcoded:** I can see your lights and your thermostat. All on one screen?
>
> **them:** epic. can we put my room on the front and everything else on a separate tab?

It rearranges.

> **them:** perfect. now make the sliders bigger and the animations punchier

They grow and bounce.

**Their money**

> **them:** can we connect my bank and show my balances in here?

Balances appear.

> **them:** can it pull out my recurring bills and stuff? is that possible?
>
> **youcoded:** Yes. Found eleven.
>
> **them:** send me those numbers every morning. make the email kawaii

A very cute email slides in.

Pull back: the app looks nothing like where it started. Then:

> stop waiting for updates.
>
> make anything you can imagine, just by asking.

Then the youcoded name, alone.

The home and money conversations are your own examples. The two closing lines are yours
too. If three conversations is too many for a minute, the first cut drops one whole
conversation rather than thinning each.

## Take 3: "Own everything"

**For:** people tired of subscriptions and of their data being someone else's. Short, for
social feeds. 20 to 30 seconds, and a poster set.

**What it is:** type only.

> you'll own nothing.
> and you'll be happy.

Hold. Then "nothing" is crossed out by hand, and "everything" is written over it.

> own your data.
> own your intelligence.
> own your software.

## Take 4: "Purple is the new orange"

**For:** people paying for one assistant app who do not know they could use any of them in
one place. 30 seconds.

**What it is:** one sentence stays on screen the whole time, and only its last word changes,
in step with the app behind it.

> purple is the new **orange**.

The app is in a warm orange look, with that company's AI model named in the app's own model
picker.

> purple is the new **green**.

The app snaps to green, the picker to the matching model.

> purple is the new **blue**.

Blue, and its model.

Then the last word spins like a wheel through more colours while the picker spins through
every family of AI model youcoded can use, faster and faster, until the whole app settles
into youcoded purple and the sentence drops away, leaving the youcoded name.

Small print: "youcoded is not affiliated with these companies." No logos; colour and mood
only.

## Take 5: "Everyone's is different"

**For:** students, small shops, families, hobbyists: people who would never call themselves
technical.

**What it is:** five people, one wish each. No faces, only a first name, what they asked
for, and their youcoded, each looking completely different from the last.

> Maya asked for her shop's orders in one place.
> Sam asked for flashcards from his lecture notes.
> Priya asked for a bedtime story with her son in it, every night.
> Tom asked for a way to track his tomatoes.
> June asked for bigger text. That's all.

Then the five sit side by side.

> nobody is the same.
>
> and neither is their youcoded.

Then the youcoded name, alone.

## Take 6 (new, from your comment): "Pass it back"

**For:** friends, housemates, couples: anyone who would send this to one other person.

**What it is:** two friends and one thing that goes back and forth between them. One makes
something for a trip they are planning and sends it over. The other adds their own part,
moves things around, and sends it back. It ends better than either would have made alone.

> **Ana:** can you make something for our road trip? stops, who's driving, what we owe
> each other
>
> *(she sends it to Jo)*
>
> **Jo:** ooh. can we add a vote for the playlist? and put the money bit at the top, ana
> always forgets
>
> *(Jo sends it back)*
>
> **Ana:** rude. keeping it

Then the youcoded name, alone.

Before I film this I have to check that sending something to a friend, and them changing it
and sending it back, works this simply in today's app. If it does not yet, this take waits.
