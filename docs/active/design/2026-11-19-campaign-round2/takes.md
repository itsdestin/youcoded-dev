# Campaign round two: the words

Five separate takes. Each is its own film for its own audience, not five cuts of one film.
Nothing here is recorded, drawn or scored yet. This is the first of your four checkpoints
(words, voice, rough cut, final).

Rules from your answers that every take follows:

- No words about code. "Make", "change" and "ask", never "build software".
- Ends on **open source everything**.
- Sixty seconds unless marked shorter.
- The app appears as cleaned-up, animated versions of things youcoded can really make.
- "youcoded" as slang (made to fit you) is shown, never explained.

---

## Take 1: "Once upon a time"

**For:** people who already feel something is wrong with big tech but have never heard there
is an alternative. The long YouTube and TV slot.

**What it is:** your script, your first half almost word for word, told as a fairytale by a
quick, mischievous British storybook voice. Your second half is replaced by three short lines.

**What you see:** a storybook drawn in our purple and plum. A few tall lit towers over a town
where every window looks the same. When "something wonderful" happens, a purple light slips
out of a tower and drifts down into the town. Window by window, each one turns into a
different youcoded: different colours, different layouts, different things on screen. The
town ends up brighter than the towers.

### Version A: names their motives (your wording)

> For more than a decade, a small handful of corporations have dictated how you interact
> with your technology.
>
> They decided what your computers could do, what it would cost, and what happened with
> your personal data.
>
> And we all... we all just accepted it. We accepted that we would subscribe to everything
> and own nothing.
>
> However, recently... something truly wonderful happened.
>
> Blinded by their own hubris, these corporations created a new kind of technology. One
> they believed would maximize shareholder value.
>
> But in their excitement, they created something far beyond their own control: a
> technology that can make software, the ever-scarce foundation of these monopolies, for
> anyone who asks.
>
> So now, your technology can finally answer to you.
>
> Ask for what you want. Change it until it fits. Share it, so everyone else can too.
>
> It's time to free ourselves from the tech monopolies of the past.
>
> It's time, at last... to open source everything.

About 165 words: a brisk minute.

### Version B: names the monopolies, not their motives

Identical, except the fifth and sixth paragraphs become:

> Racing each other, these corporations created a new kind of technology.
>
> And in that race, they made something far beyond their own control: a technology that
> can make software, the ever-scarce foundation of these monopolies, for anyone who asks.

**What changed from your script, and why:** "Artificial General Intelligence" is gone (your
answer). "Build software" became "make software". "Rarely stopping to consider the
consequences for those who might be harmed" is cut for time. The "imagine a world" half is
replaced, because you said you don't love it.

**Without a narrator:** the same words as large type, a line at a time, over the same
pictures. I would make both so you can compare.

## Take 2: "Make it mine"

**For:** people who already use ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini every day and have never imagined
they could change the app itself.

**What it is:** no narrator. One person talking to their youcoded, and it changing in front
of them. Fast, cut to the music. The requests go from small to surprising, and each is a
different kind of thing: a look, a connection to something they own, a daily job, a toy.

**What you see and read** (each line is typed by the person; the app answers by changing):

1. "make this feel like a rainy night in tokyo" — the whole app turns to neon and rain.
2. "can you run my lights from here?" — a set of light sliders appears.
3. "epic. my room on the front, the rest of the house on another tab" — it rearranges.
4. "bigger sliders. punchier." — they grow and bounce.
5. "every morning, email me what's on today. make it cute." — a small, very cute email
   slides in.
6. "my niece likes frogs. make her a game." — a frog game, being played.
7. Pull back: the finished app looks nothing like where it started.

Then three cards of type:

> nobody made this for you.
>
> you asked for it.
>
> open source everything.

**Why these examples:** none is "a page for X". Each one is something a ready-made app
would not do the way this person wants it. The lights are there once, as one request among
six, and the point is the back-and-forth, as in your own example.

## Take 3: "Own everything"

**For:** people tired of subscriptions and of their data being someone else's. Short, for
social feeds. 20 to 30 seconds, and a poster set.

**What it is:** type only, no app until the last second.

> you'll own nothing.
> and you'll be happy.

Hold. Then "nothing" is crossed out by hand, and "everything" is written over it.

> own your data.
> own your intelligence.
> own your software.
> own everything.
>
> open source everything.

**Posters from the same idea:**

- "you'll own ~~nothing~~ everything. and you'll be happy."
- A set of four: "own your data." / "own your intelligence." / "own your software." /
  "own everything."

## Take 4: "Purple is the new orange"

**For:** people paying for one assistant app who do not know they could use any of them in
one place. 30 seconds.

**What it is:** your idea. The app flashes through looks that feel like each of the big
assistants (by colour and mood only, no logos), and each time the name of the matching AI
model is showing in the app's own model picker. Then a fast wheel of every family of model
youcoded can use. It lands on purple.

> orange.
> green.
> blue.
>
> or all of them.
>
> purple is the new orange.
>
> open source everything.

Small print: "youcoded is not affiliated with these companies."

## Take 5: "Everyone's is different"

**For:** students, small shops, families, hobbyists: people who would never call themselves
technical. This is the take that says youcoded is made by everyone who uses it.

**What it is:** five people, one wish each. We never see faces, only a first name, what they
asked for, and their youcoded, each looking completely different from the last.

> Maya asked for her shop's orders in one place.
> Sam asked for flashcards from his lecture notes.
> Priya asked for a bedtime story with her son in it, every night.
> Tom asked for a way to track his tomatoes.
> June asked for bigger text. That's all.

Then the five sit side by side, and more appear around them until the screen is full.

> nobody's is the same.
>
> and anything one person makes, everyone can use.
>
> open source everything.

The names are invented. If you would rather use real people's real requests, this take can
wait for them.
