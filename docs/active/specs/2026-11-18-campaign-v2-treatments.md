# youcoded campaign, round 2: three directions

## What went wrong in round 1

- **The idea was a pun on the name.** "you coded that." tells a non-technical person this is an
  app for coders, which is exactly the person we are not talking to. The word "code" now appears
  nowhere in the ads except inside the logo.
- **It was a product demo.** Sixty seconds of a chat window doing things. It showed features and
  never showed why anyone should care. The vision (own your technology, stop renting your life,
  anyone can build) was missing.
- **The craft gap.** Every frame was a flat app window on a pale background, with music I wrote
  in code and never heard. Big-company ads get their feeling from three things we did not have:
  a real piece of music, a human voice or presence, and a world around the screen.

## Direction A: "Yours." (recommended for the 60 s, 30 s and shorts)

**Feeling:** fed up, then defiant, then free. It's Destin's "it is time to fight back", without
being doom-y.

| Time | Picture | Words on screen (and voice, if any) |
|---|---|---|
| 0–10 s | Dark plum. One word at a time, cold and clipped, speeding up with the music until they pile into a wall. | Sign in. · Subscribe. · Renew. · Upgrade to continue. · Accept the new terms. · Your price has changed. · Your data is shared with our partners. |
| 10–13 s | Everything cuts out. Silence. | Everything became a subscription. |
| 13–14 s | One beat. | Not anymore. |
| 14–44 s | Light floods in and the music opens up. The app runs on a laptop, a phone and a tablet, shown as real devices floating in space instead of flat windows. Someone asks for things in plain words, and each thing appears. | Your home. · Your money. · Your look. · Your rules. (One word-pair per thing, each on the beat.) |
| 44–52 s | The devices drift together. | Just ask for it. · And it's yours. |
| 52–60 s | End card. | youcoded · Free · Windows · Mac · Linux · Android · open source everything |

- **30 s:** the same shape, compressed, with three things instead of five.
- **Shorts (vertical, 15 s):** each one is one moment, e.g. "Cancel the smart-home app." then
  the house page appears, then "Yours." Then the logo.
- **Posters:** a single bold line, each paired with one real result:
  - "Your home. Not theirs."
  - "Stop renting your life."
  - "Ask. Own it."
  - "Everything you pay for monthly, built for you, free."

**Why I recommend it:**
- It sells the vision (ownership and freedom), not features.
- It never asks the viewer to understand technology.
- It has a real emotional turn, which is what makes an ad memorable.

**Risk:** the opening has to stay wry, not angry, or it tips into "doom-y". I'd keep it short.

**Claims check:**
- Free, open source, data on your own devices, works on Windows, Mac, Linux and Android: all true
  today.
- "Built for you" means made by asking. That's true.

## Direction B: "Built by everyone."

**Feeling:** wonder at scale.

- One person asks for something small, like a recipe page. They share it. Someone in another city
  changes it, someone else gives it a new look, and it spreads.
- **Line:** "Most apps are built by a company. This one is built by everyone who uses it."

**Pro:** it is the most original idea here. Nobody else can say it.

**Cons:**
- It's harder to film honestly. We can't claim a big community yet (brand §10), so it has to read
  as a promise, not a boast.
- It's better as a second-wave ad, once the marketplace has more homegrown things to show.

## Direction C: "Destin." (a 90 s–2 min film for YouTube, not TV)

**Feeling:** one real person's story.

- Destin, in his own voice: "I'm not a programmer. This year I left Google. Then Microsoft. ..."
  We show the things he really replaced: his office suite, his smart home, his money tracking,
  and the swipe keyboard he got built in days.
- **Line:** his own words, quoted, never paraphrased (brand §9).

**Pro:** the brand guide calls his story our strongest material. It's authentic in a way no
polished ad can fake.

**Con:** it needs his voice and his time, and it's long. It's for YouTube and the website, not
paid TV slots.

## What polish needs (whichever direction)

| Piece | Option | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Music** | Code-composed (as round 1) | Free, owned. Most likely the weakest part. |
| | Licensed track from a real composer (Musicbed, Artlist, ~$15–30 a month or a one-off licence) | The biggest single jump in feel. Costs money. Needs your account. Breaks the brand rule "music composed in code". |
| **Voice** | None, words on screen only | Works muted (most feeds play muted). No casting. Less warmth. |
| | Destin reads 6–8 lines (phone in a quiet room, ~15 min) | The most authentic. Your voice is the brand. |
| | Hired voice actor (~$100–400) | Smooth and professional. Can feel generic. |
| **Picture** | The app on rendered devices in a lavender space, with depth, light and shadow | Gives a sense of a world. No fake interface; it is real footage on device shapes. |
| | Stock footage of people's lives | Not recommended: it reads as stock. It's the cliché risk Destin named. |
