# Perf cycle 3 — bounding the conversation window

> **The branch name is a fossil.** Eviction was specced, reviewed and REJECTED;
> what shipped folds entries instead of deleting them. §2 is the record of that
> decision, kept because the reasons are the most reusable thing here.

## 1. What was broken, measured

Cycle 2 made a conversation *open* at its last 30 turns. It did not bound how large
one can *become*. A page loaded by scrolling up is prepended and nothing removes it,
and every open session keeps a mounted ChatView. Baseline, three repeats, six
sessions, each resumed conversation scrolled to its beginning:

| | floor (nothing scrolled) | ceiling (read to the top) |
|---|---|---|
| PSS | 1,539 MB | **4,306 MB** |
| DOM nodes | ~23,500 | **+1,441,256** (~119 per message) |
| freed by switching away + a forced GC | — | **41 MB — 1.5%** |

And it was felt: within one `huge` leg the cost of the next page rose **201 ms →
705 ms** as the app filled up, and the 50-turn `small` conversation posted the
slowest single page of the run (1,001 ms). **The cost tracked what the app held, not
what the conversation held.**

## 2. Three approaches, two closed by evidence in this repo

**(a) `content-visibility: auto` — CLOSED, already tried and reverted.** `globals.css`
records it above `.timeline-entry { contain: layout style; }`: its implicit
`contain: paint` clips the box-shadow glows community themes draw. The remaining win
needs `paint`, and `paint` is what breaks themes.

**(b) Evicting entries from the reducer — SPECCED, then REJECTED on independent
review (2026-08-28).** Six blocking defects, four of them silent. The two that killed
it outright:
- Deleting a turn leaves its uuids in `seenUuids`, so scrolling back replays a page
  whose every event is dropped as already-seen — **the messages are gone for the
  session**. Clearing those uuids instead makes `mergeTotals` re-add the page's
  tokens on every re-fetch.
- ~15 readers fold the whole session-lifetime `toolCalls` / `toolGroups` /
  `assistantTurns` maps. The review found the spec's own four-name suspect list was
  wrong on three of four. Real casualties: the specialists ledger (a running
  specialist vanishes and its permission asks become unanswerable), `/copy N`
  returning a different turn, `/usage` reporting stale figures, and an assistant turn
  whose record is missing rendering as **a silent hole with no error**.

The review also killed the spec's stated reason for preferring (b) over (c): eviction
removes the same text from the DOM, so it inherits the identical find-in-chat
limitation. Find-scope does not discriminate between them.

**(c) FOLDING — BUILT.** An entry more than ~1.5 screens outside the viewport renders
as a spacer of the height its body last occupied. The timeline entry, its reducer
records and its uuids all stay. Scroll height never changes, nothing is re-fetched,
unfolding is a render rather than a disk read, and every defect in (b) is structurally
absent because nothing is deleted.

Unlike (b), folding can also make find whole: the text is still in memory, so opening
the find bar unfolds everything for its duration.

## 2b. What it actually cost — measured, 3 repeats each, same instrument

Baseline `29658c5` vs `perf/evict-offscreen-turns`, every phase:

| | before | after | |
|---|---|---|---|
| ceiling PSS | 4,345.6 MB | **1,826.5 MB** | −58.0% |
| growth from scrolling | +2,788.2 MB | +305.5 MB | −89.0% |
| DOM nodes added | +1,441,274 | **−8,517** | eliminated |
| JS heap added | 513.2 MB | 64.1 MB | −87.5% |
| session switch p95 | 242.6 ms | 112.3 ms | −53.7% |
| switch into `huge` | 201.2 ms | 77.8 ms | −61.3% |
| main-thread blocking | 4,882 ms | 1,703 ms | −65.1% |
| page turn, medium / small | 885 / 1,000 ms | 373 / 133 ms | −58% / −87% |

Flat (no regression): startup ≤1.8%, idle PSS +0.1%, history resume ≤0.3%, stall
identical, artifacts ≤2.4%. Screenshots 4/5 byte-identical; `native-chat` differs
6.38%, which is **less than the 14.79% two runs of the SAME baseline produce** — its
body is generated by the local model. Recorded in `screenshots.mjs` →
`NONDETERMINISTIC_SCREENS`.

The JS-heap cut was not predicted: folding was expected to recover only the DOM.
React's fibers for an unmounted body go with it.

## 2c. Three bugs found only by measurement — the reusable part

Each looked correct in review and each was caught by a number, after a wrong guess:

1. **Ref churn.** `attachEntry` depended on the whole `folding` object, a fresh
   literal every render, so React detached and re-attached the ref on all 7,000
   entries every render — and each re-attach restarted the fold idle timer, so
   folding never fired. It measured **200 MB WORSE than doing nothing**.
2. **A drained queue.** `flushFold` drained a pending set and cleared it, dropping
   entries with no measured height. They never transition again, so they were
   permanently unfoldable.
3. **Requiring a prior sighting.** Heights were recorded only when an entry reported
   *visible*, which required it to have passed within 1,500 px of the viewport. A page
   prepends ~60 entries at once and only the top few land in that band. Fixed by
   measuring the element at fold time.

**All three presented identically: ~730 of 12,100 entries folded (6%), stable across
three different builds.** The aggregate could not distinguish them. What broke the
loop was a **per-pane** count (433/7000, 294/5000, 1/100) — one run, one answer.
The rig now carries it, plus a settle window before every reading, because a
viewport-driven optimisation is lazy by construction and reading immediately after
the last scroll measures the app mid-transition.

## 3. What was built

**`use-observed-ref.ts` — the prerequisite.** `observeEntry` observed every timeline
entry and nothing ever called `unobserve` (verified repo-wide; the only matches were
three test mocks). An `IntersectionObserver` holds a STRONG reference to its targets,
so the first change to remove or replace an entry would have freed nothing at all,
silently, with every test green. Now an ast-grep rule
(`observer-ref-returns-cleanup.yml`) catches the shape repo-wide.

The hook ALWAYS returns a cleanup. React 19 picks the detach convention per call:
return a function and it runs that; return `undefined` and React calls the ref with
`null`. Observers are built in effects, which run AFTER refs attach, so an early
`undefined` on the first render put that ref on the null-call convention and the next
detach passed `null` into `observe()`. Five existing ChatView tests caught it.

**`use-entry-folding.ts` — the change.** One extra `IntersectionObserver` at
`rootMargin: 1500px 0px`, holding out-of-view MEMBERSHIP (never a drained queue).
Folding waits 800 ms of scroll-idle; unfolding runs on a 100 ms debounce. Asymmetric
on purpose: folding mid-scroll re-renders the list under a moving viewport, which is
the judder Destin reported by feel in cycle 2. Heights are measured from the live
element at fold time, falling back to a remembered height when the element measures 0
— that fallback is what makes a background session foldable at all, since its pane is
`content-visibility: hidden` and its children lay out to 0. An entry with no height
from either source is NEVER folded: a 0 px spacer would collapse the scroll height
under the reader.

`ChatView.tsx` renders `{folded && foldHeight ? null : content}` on a wrapper carrying
`style={{ height: foldHeight }}` and `data-entry-key`. Folding is suspended and
everything unfolded while the find bar is open.

## 4. Still open

- **Not eyeballed.** Fast scrolling and switching between conversations are where a
  blank frame would show, and both are cursor/timing sensitive. Destin's call, not a rig's.
- **Find-in-chat only searches loaded messages** — TRUE ON MASTER TODAY as a
  consequence of cycle-2 paging, surfaced by this cycle's review and NOT yet fixed.
  A conversation with `history.hasMore` should say so beside the match count rather
  than reporting `0/0` for text the user can see. Filed to the roadmap.
- **Collapse/expand state is per-card `useState`**, so a card that folds and unfolds
  loses a manual toggle. Judged acceptable (the review agreed): lifting it into the
  reducer would add per-card state to the object this cycle exists to shrink.
- Android on-device paging, and the §4 smaller readers from the cycle-2 spec.

## 5. What NOT to try again
- `content-visibility: auto` on `.timeline-entry` — see §2(a).
- Deleting entries from the reducer — see §2(b). The six blocking defects are in the
  review record; the two fatal ones are structural, not implementation bugs.
- Judging a lazy, viewport-driven optimisation from an aggregate metric or from an
  immediate post-scroll reading — see §2c. Three separate bugs presented as the same
  6%.
