# The youcoded songbook

## What this is

Every advert, film and clip gets its music from this one songbook, so all our music is
recognisably one family, the way a film's theme comes back in every scene. **What is fixed is
the identity:** one sound ("felt and pulse": a soft, close felt piano is the person; clean, quiet
plucked and held strings and a soft synth are the machine doing what the person asks), one key
(E-flat major and its relative C minor), one tune built on three notes in the rhythm of
"you-co-ded", one family of chords, four ways of accompanying, and the sound logo as every
piece's last phrase.

**What is not fixed is the music.** Until 2026-11-23 this page said "nobody writes new music for a
new video; a new piece is made by listing sections". That produced "Make it mine": the tune's
opening bar seven times in 54 s over one four-chord loop, never resting, its payoff quieter than
the bar before it. The owner: "extremely repetitive and poorly put together". So the rule is now
the one every good film composer works by: **the theme is fixed, the score is composed.** Each
film gets its own piece, written for its picture, that *develops* the tune (fragments it, moves
it, answers it, holds it back) instead of restating it. The chapter "Composing a score" says how;
"The hard limits" are checked automatically on every render; a piece that breaks one FAILS.

The blocks come from the three pieces the owner said yes to: "One becomes many" (blend-1),
"Pizzicato bounce" (chamber-1) and "Rocking piano, singing strings" (chamber-2). Nothing comes
from the versions he said no to (both two-piano versions and "Mirror").

## The tune

Key: E-flat major, like everything before. One bar is four beats.

| Bar | Notes | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **B-flat, G, F**, E-flat | the signature "you-co-ded", then one step further down |
| 2 | E-flat, high C, B-flat, G | the same shape, higher |
| 3 | A-flat, G, E-flat, C | falls back down |
| 4 | E-flat, then a long D | hangs open, like a question |
| 5-6 | the same as bars 1-2 | |
| 7 | high C, B-flat, A-flat, G | from the top |
| 8 | F, G, A-flat, B-flat | climbs back up toward home |
| ending | high C (long), B-flat (long) | a sigh onto B-flat, the logo's own first note |

The rhythm is mostly "long, long, longer, short": the short note pushes into the next bar,
which gives it a little skip.

**Why this tune.** It is the one chamber-1 and chamber-2 share, so two of the three pieces you
liked are already built on it, and it has what the rules ask for: a real tune that grows out
of the three signature notes instead of repeating them. blend-1's own tune was the three
notes said three times, each a step higher; you have said tunes should be more interesting than
that. What made blend-1 special was its *conversation* (a second piano answering every
phrase), so that became its own block, THE ANSWER, re-written to fit under this tune.

It can be used three ways: **in full** (8 bars), **just the first phrase** (bars 1-4, which
ends on a question) or **just the signature** (the three notes alone, then a bar of space:
for under a narrator).

## The blocks

**THE ANSWER** (from blend-1). A second piano, on the other side, answers the tune. Since
2026-11-23 it *listens*: it speaks only where the tune leaves at least a beat of space, and it
answers with the tune's own last two or three notes (an echo an octave down; every other answer
upside down), landing on a note of the chord. Where the tune leaves no space it waits, so a tune
that never rests gets no answer: write rests. (The pieces made before then keep the old answer,
a fixed three-note ladder on beats 3, 3.5 and 4, which talked over the tune.) It stops while the
violin has the tune (one voice at a time).

**THE CHORDS.** One home progression, in two forms (the wider vocabulary a score may use is in
"Composing a score", Harmony):

- *Bright* (E-flat major): E-flat | C minor | A-flat | B-flat. The brand's chords.
- *Dark* (for a problem, a flat moment): C minor | A-flat | F minor | G. C minor is E-flat
  major's "relative minor": it uses exactly the same seven notes. So **the dark form changes
  not one note of the tune**; only the floor under it turns sad. The one new note, B natural,
  lives only in the accompaniment's G chord, which gives the minor key its pull.
- *The turn*: the dark form's last bar rises A-flat, B-flat, the classic way out of a minor
  key. Put it at the end of a dark section and the next bright section feels like the sun
  coming out, not like a change of record.

**THE ACCOMPANIMENT** (the engine under the tune; it never stops *by accident*, and works at any
speed; since 2026-11-23 each pattern can be thinned, halved, dropped or rested bar by bar, see
"Composing a score"):

- **Rolling piano** (blend-1): the left hand rolls through each chord in even notes. Warm,
  flowing; pedalled in the warm moods.
- **Pizzicato bounce** (chamber-1): cellos pluck the low note, violas pluck little chords in
  between the beats, a soft piano chord underneath. Light and bouncy.
- **Rocking piano** (chamber-2): the left hand rocks low-high with a skip, cellos plucking the
  low note under it. Warm and lyrical.
- **Sparse** (new, for stark moments): one low open chord held under each chord, and a single
  note (G) pulsing slowly twice a bar, whatever the chord. Deadpan.
- (**Still**, for a hush: one soft rolled chord, touched again halfway. Barely moving.)

**THE LAYERS** (switched on or off per section):

- **Second-piano answers** (THE ANSWER, above).
- **Plucked strings**: cellos and violas plucking along with the accompaniment's rhythm.
- **Held strings**: three low bowed notes that move as little as possible; they never swell
  and dip, they only change level in long straight lines.
- **Solo violin takes the tune** (chamber-2): the violin sings it, the violin section doubles
  it quietly, the cellos sing it an octave lower. When the tune passes from piano to violin,
  the piano plays the first bar with the violin, softly, then lets go (you hear the hand-over).
- **Both**: the violin sings the tune while the piano plays it in octaves (the biggest).
- **Soft synth**: quietly doubles the accompaniment's moving line; its tone opens a little
  across a section (never a sweep).
- **Soft sub bass**: a round low note under each chord; weight, no rhythm. It fades in over a
  bar when it first arrives.
- **Low cellos**: hold each chord's lowest note (part of the lift; on in triumphant).
- **Air**: one high, quiet, held violin line (E-flat, moving to D only on the "leaning" chord).
  It is continuous, never an accent. It is there so the music is not dull on bright speakers.

**THE LIFT** (how a section grows into a big moment, using only the playing): across the
section everyone plays a little harder in one straight line (about 1.5 dB by its end), the held
strings rise further (about 3.5 dB), the cellos join low underneath and the high violins join
halfway (the sound gets wider at both ends), and the piano doubles the tune an octave lower.
The lift's level then stays up through the next section. No risers, no whooshes, no drums.

**TRANSITIONS** between sections (always musical):

- **Blend** (the default whenever the mood changes; revised after your listen): the change is
  prepared over the last **two bars** of the outgoing section. The next section's accompaniment
  grows in underneath (from about a third to nearly full strength) while the old one thins and
  stops exactly at the bar line, so the new feel is already moving when its section starts. If
  the speed changes, the players speed up or broaden gradually across those two bars (never in
  one bar, never in a jump). Strings the new section adds enter during those bars from nothing;
  strings it drops bow away there. A held string note common to both sides is held straight
  through the join (one bow). If the tune changes hands (piano to violin), the new voice joins
  the tune in the last bar before the join. Everyone's loudness moves in one straight line
  across the two bars, so there is no dip. New sections start on the tune's phrase boundary.
- **Build**: a blend that also grows louder into the next section.
- **Flow**: the same mood carrying on: the next section simply starts on the next bar.
- **Hold** and **Breath** (no longer defaults): a held chord, or a pause where the last notes
  ring. Your notes on the journey showed a held chord between two moods sounds like the music
  stopped and restarted; keep them for a deliberate, deadpan pause (stark-to-bright uses one).

**THE ENDINGS**:

The logo is no longer a file pasted after the music (revised after your listen: the pasted file
ran at its own speed after pieces at other speeds, so you heard the song stop and the logo start).
**The band now plays the logo as the song's own last phrase**, in the song's own tempo:

- **The cadence**: a last bar of A-flat then B-flat, slowing gradually across the bar (its end
  is about 85 % of the song's speed). The tune sighs high C, B-flat; the band holds the B-flat
  chord; then the logo's own three notes, B-flat, G, F, are played as the tune's last words:
  two quick pick-up notes and the F landing on the next downbeat, on E-flat. The name appears
  exactly on that F (measured within 2.3 ms of the requested time in every file).
- **Full-blend ending** (any piece with music): the same notes, loudness and instruments as the
  chosen full-blend logo: the piano with plucked violins on the three notes, a low E-flat on the
  piano with a plucked cello and the soft sub under the landing, the held strings opening into a
  warm swell, and the second piano answering low (B-flat, G, F). Everything then rings and
  settles; the last second fades to silence.
- **Piano-only ending**: the same bar on the piano alone (the strings bow away across the bar),
  then the three notes and the open low E-flat chord of the chosen piano-only logo.
- **The logo files** are now used only when the logo plays on its own (`out/logo-alone.wav`).

## The moods

| Mood | Sounds like | Use it for | Usual speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stark** | the dark chords, the sparse pattern, the tune played plainly and dry, a low held string bed | a problem, a flat statement, an awkward silence | 66-100 beats a minute (80) |
| **Hush** | the piano almost still, far strings | the quiet moment between two feelings | 66-100 (80) |
| **Hopeful** | bright chords, rolling piano, held strings, air | under a narrator; a hopeful turn; slow builds | 72-96 (84) |
| **Playful** | pizzicato bounce, the answers, quick | product moments, things working | 108-128 (120) |
| **Triumphant** | rocking piano, violin and piano in octaves, everything on | the final line, the payoff, before the logo | 96-126 (112) |

Any section can add or drop single layers, swap the accompaniment, or set its own speed.

## The rules (the owner's rulings, in his words where possible)

- **No drums, claps or clacks, ever**, no kick and no kit. Energy comes from instruments playing
  together. (Nothing drum-like exists in the songbook's code.)
- **Never a fake-out or a "thwip"**: "it sounds like a 2005 browser demo switching pages". No
  stops answered by a flourish; no zips, sweeps, whooshes or risers.
- **One piece of music per film**: "we cant *just* have notes, we need consistent
  tunes/strings/chords/etc." One song with a few spotted turns of feeling, never a new cue per shot.
- **No scattered high jingles.** A high part is a continuous line (the air layer, a held violin).
- **Under a narrator, the music's level does not bounce.** It is set by hand per section and
  changes only slowly, never with an automatic dip on every word. *This ruling is about ducking
  under a voice; it is not a rule against dynamics.* Musical swells and falls of 3-6 dB across
  phrases are wanted (see "Dynamics").
- **Music answers feelings; sound effects answer actions.** The music never adds an accent for a
  word or an action.
- **Sound effects are made for each film, never taken from a shared kit**, and only where that
  film needs them. **Pen sounds are soft and smooth, never scratchy** ("annoying and too
  scratchy"; "Soft pen" was his pick).
- **The voice always wins.**
- **Two logos**: "piano only if we're ever using the logo sound by itself without other music,
  but the full blend if at the end of an ad with our other music."
- **Subtle when asked.** On "Own everything": "the music should be a little more subtle".
  Subtle means fewer notes, softer touch, more space and one clear idea, not the same
  arrangement turned down.

## Composing a score

Read this whole chapter before writing a note. Every rule ends in a number that
`render.sh --notes <film>` checks in about a second (see "The hard limits").

### 1. Plan the whole arc from the picture, before any notes

1. **Spot it.** Watch the cut. Mark the 2-4 moments where the *feeling* changes (the problem,
   the turn, the payoff) and the one moment that is **the peak**. Everything else (cuts, typing,
   a new screen) the music does not follow.
2. **Draw the arc in four numbers per passage**: how loud (quiet / middle / full), how high (the
   register the tune sits in), how busy (notes a bar), and what the tune is doing (absent,
   fragment, developed, stated). The arc must *go somewhere*: at least one valley before the peak,
   the peak higher, louder and fuller than anything before it, then a settling into the logo.
   A typical 60 s arc: quiet and sparse (fragments) → a first statement → a contrast passage
   (new chords, the tune elsewhere or resting) → a thinning ("drop before the lift") → the peak
   (the full statement, highest register, loudest bar) → the logo.
3. **Write through, not in loops.** A section is not a block to be repeated; it is the next
   sentence of one story. Never copy a passage and switch a layer on to make the "next" one:
   *adding an instrument is not development* (the checks ignore held, air, sub and synth layers
   when deciding whether a bar is new).
4. **Name the peak**: `Piece(peak=46.0)`. The checks then hold the score to it.

### 2. Phrases and how they answer each other

- **Two-bar ideas, four-bar phrases.** A phrase is two short ideas: a *call* (bars 1-2) and a
  *response* (bars 3-4). The response varies the call (same rhythm, new pitches; or same start,
  new ending); it never repeats it.
- **Phrases end; they are not cut.** End every phrase on a deliberate arrival: *open* (on
  B-flat or D, over B-flat: a question), *closed* (on E-flat, over E-flat: home) or *deceptive*
  (expecting E-flat, landing on C minor). Vary the endings: never three open endings in a row,
  and at least one closed arrival before the logo in any film over 30 s. Never shorten a phrase
  with `"phrase1:3"` to fit the picture; write a 3-bar phrase that ends, or change the tempo.
- **Phrase lengths may vary**: 4 + 4, then 3 + 5 (a phrase stretched by one bar of answer or
  of held note), or a 2-bar phrase repeated a step higher (a sequence) to build tension.
- **Two phrases answer each other by contrast in one dimension at a time**: the second higher,
  or with the rhythm turned around, or over new chords, never all three at once (that is a new
  tune, not an answer).

### 3. The tune: an identity, not wallpaper

The three notes "you-co-ded" (B-flat, up to G, down to F) are the brand. Hearing them is the
point; hearing them *identically* again and again is a jingle.

- **The full opening bar, exactly as written**: at most once per 30 s, and never in a film under
  20 s (there the logo at the end *is* the statement).
- **The cell (any three notes with the shape "up a sixth, down a step")**: at least once before
  the logo in any piece over 8 s; never more than 3 times in the identical form (same pitches,
  same rhythm) within 30 s.
- **No bar of the tune more than twice unchanged.** The third time, develop it.
- **Ways to develop it** (each is one line of code, `src/motif.py`; chain them):

| Device | Code | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Fragment: just the cell, or the tune's last notes | `motif("signature")`, `motif("bar3").take(0, 2)` | busy picture, under a voice, a hint |
| Sequence: the cell again a step higher | `motif("signature").seq(1, 3, every=2)` | building tension into a turn |
| Move it along the scale (stays in key) | `.up(2)`, `.down(1)` | a second phrase that answers the first |
| New register | `.octave(-1)` (cellos, low piano), `.octave(1)` | contrast; save the top register for the peak |
| Augmentation: twice as slow | `.aug(2)` | a held string line under the piano; a calm |
| Diminution | `.dim(2)` | a playful quick echo |
| Inversion: upside down | `.invert()` | the answer; a darker turn |
| Delayed entry | `.shift(0.5)` | the tune "breathing in" after a rest; syncopation |
| New ending | `.end_on("Eb5", 2)` | closed / open / deceptive phrase endings |
| New rhythm, same notes | `.rhythm([1.5, 0.5, 2])` | the same words said with a new accent |
| Passing notes | `.fill()` | an ornament the second time round |
| Touch per note | `.touch(1.1, 0.8, 0.9)` | shaping a phrase by hand |
| Written by hand | `write("Bb4/1 G5/1 F5/2 \| r/2 Eb5/1 D5/1")` | a counter-line, a new B phrase |

- **A film over 30 s needs a second idea**: a B phrase (the tune's rhythm on new notes, or a
  4-bar answer phrase written by hand) or a counter-line (`Section.counter`), so the tune's
  return is felt as a return.
- **A film over 30 s uses at least three guises** of the tune (e.g. fragment, sequence, full).

### 4. Harmony: harmonic rhythm and the wider vocabulary

- The home progression (E-flat, C minor, A-flat, B-flat) is the brand's floor. **It may not loop
  more than once in a row** (the chords of a bar may equal those four bars earlier for at most
  four bars running).
- **Vocabulary** (all in `src/tune.py`, every one voiced for every pattern):
  - diatonic: `Eb`, `Cm7`, `Cm`, `Abmaj7`, `Ab`, `Fm7`, `Fm`, `Gm7`, `Bb`, `Bbsus`, `Bb7`,
    `Ab/Bb` (a soft dominant);
  - inversions, so the bass can walk by step: `Eb/G`, `Eb/Bb`, `Eb/D`, `Cm7/Bb`, `Ab/C`,
    `Fm/Ab`, `Bb/D`, `Cm/G`;
  - borrowed colours, **once per film, as a surprise**: `Abm` (iv: bittersweet, the turn
    before home), `Db` (bVII: "sun through clouds", lovely straight into E-flat), `F7` (V of
    V: a brighter push to B-flat), `G` (the dark form's pull). Their new note clashes with the
    tune's A-flat (F7), C (A-flat minor) or D (D-flat): the check `clashes` fails any tune note
    on that clash.
- **Distinct chords**: at least `min(7, 3 + seconds / 15)` (10 s → 3, 30 s → 5, 60 s → 7).
- **Harmonic rhythm**: change it at least once per minute: two chords in a bar (a bar written
  `["Ab", "Bbsus"]`, two beats each) to push into a peak; one chord held for two bars (the same
  chord in two bars) for a calm or under a voice.
- **Cadences**: vary them (see phrase endings). A deceptive cadence (B-flat → C minor) is the
  cheapest way to keep a 60 s piece going without a full stop.
- `tune.smooth_bass(["Eb", "Cm7", "Ab", "Bb", ...])` picks inversions so the bass moves by step;
  use it, then hand-edit.

### 5. Bass and inner voices

- **The bass is a line, not a list of roots.** At least once in every 8 bars it plays a note that
  is not the chord's root (an inversion, a passing note or a pedal). Tools: `Section.bass=`
  `"walk"` (two scale steps into each new phrase), `"pedal:Eb2"` (one held bass note under
  moving chords: suspense, a build), or a `write(...)` Phrase (the bass line itself).
- **Walk into a section change** (`into="pickup"`) instead of jumping to it.
- **Inner voices move by step or not at all.** The held strings already do (they keep a common
  note through a chord change); a counter-line should too: mostly steps, one leap per phrase,
  moving when the tune holds and holding when the tune moves.
- **The counter-line** (`Section.counter=write(...)`, `counter_voice="cello"`, `"viola"`,
  `"violin"`, `"piano"` or `"pizz"`) lives below the tune (cello: G2-G3, viola: C3-C4) or far
  above it as a held line; never in the tune's own octave.

### 6. Register and texture plan

- **Save the top.** The tune's highest note (C6 in the written tune) and the loudest bar belong
  to the peak: not before one bar ahead of it. Earlier statements sit lower (`.down(2)`,
  `.octave(-1)`) or are fragments that stop short of the top.
- **Each turn changes at least two of**: register, density, timbre (who plays), harmony,
  loudness. Adding a quiet layer alone does not count.
- **The accompaniment develops.** Within any 6 bars it plays at least one rhythm it has not
  played in the 8 bars before. Tools, bar by bar: `vary={4: "thin", 8: "drop"}`
  (`"thin"` = bass and downbeat chord only; `"half"` = beats 1 and 3 only; `"drop"` = stops at
  beat 3, the last thing rings; `"bass"` = bass alone; `"rest"` = silence under the tune;
  `"full"`), `density=0.6` (the weakest eighths leave first), `register=+12` (the chords move
  up an octave, bass unchanged), or a change of pattern.
- **Hand over, never pile up.** A blend may not have more than 1.2 × the notes of the music on
  either side (the v2 blend thins the outgoing pattern and grows the incoming one from its strong
  beats).

### 7. Rests, breath and silence

- **The tune rests at least one bar in every 8** (no tune note starting). That bar is where the
  answer, a counter-line or the accompaniment alone speaks, and it is what makes the next entry
  an event.
- **Drop before the lift.** Before the peak, thin to one or two instruments for at least half a
  bar (`vary={n: "drop"}` or `"bass"`, `into="drop"`, or `shape="drop-lift"`). The peak arrives
  out of space, not out of clutter.
- **Silence is allowed** for a beat or two at a story turn (`vary={n: "rest"}` with the tune
  resting too), never as a fake-out: the music continues on the same pulse.

### 8. Dynamics over the whole piece

- **A shape, not a ramp.** Per-bar loudness must move at least 4 dB across a piece of 20 s or more
  (3 dB under 20 s) and not more than 14 dB. Use `Section.shape`: `"swell"`, `"fade"`, `"arch"`,
  `"grow"`, `"drop-lift"`, `"inward"`, or your own `[(beat, level), ...]` (two points on the same
  beat make a sudden change, a *subito*).
- **The peak arrives**: its two bars at least 2 dB louder than the 4 bars before it (2 bars in a
  film under 15 s), and the loudest bar of the piece within a bar before to two bars after the
  peak, or the logo.
- **After the peak, let it settle** (`shape="fade"` on the last bars) so the logo lands into
  space.

### 9. Turning the mood without a jolt

- One turn = one musical event, prepared: a blend over 1-2 bars (`into="blend"`/`"build"`) when
  the accompaniment changes; `into="pickup"` (the bass walks in) or `into="drop"` (the last half
  bar thins) when it does not.
- The new section starts on a **new chord or a new register**, not just a new layer.
- Keep one thing constant across the join (a held string note, the pulse, the tune's last note
  becoming the new section's first) so the turn feels like the same song changing its mind.

### 10. Ending

- The logo is the last phrase (B-flat, G, then F lands on the name). The bar before it should
  *lead into it*: a closed or open cadence prepared by the arc, not a phrase cut off.
- **Do not pin `ending_start`** unless the picture demands it. Pinning it and the landing forces
  the last bar to start faster (the old films lurch +4 % into the ending). Pin only `land`; the
  fitting then slows into the logo with no step. Check `tempo-steps` (≤ 2 %).

### 11. Timing and touch (humanising)

- Every note is already a little early or late (5-7 ms) and a little softer or harder (3 %).
  Beyond that, write it:
  - `broaden=0.02`: the last two beats of a section slow by 2 % (a singer taking time), then
    the next section is back in tempo. Use at phrase ends before a turn, 1-3 %.
  - `push=-8` (ms): the tune leans ahead of the beat (eager, playful); `push=+10`: laid back.
  - `legato=0.15` (beats): the tune's notes overlap a little (singing); `pedal=False`: dry.
  - `lead=0.85`: the tune a little more inward against the band (subtle films).
  - per note: `.touch(...)`, or `@0.8` in `write()`.
- No speed change bigger than 2 % from one beat to the next except into a held chord or after a
  deliberate broadening.

### 12. Under a narrator

- Mark where she speaks: `Piece(speech=films.speech_from_words(<words.json>, t_from, t_to))`
  (spans of words, joined across pauses under 0.3 s) and `narration=True` on those sections.
- **No melody in her range while she speaks**: no tune, answer or counter-line note between F3
  and E5 may *start* inside a spoken span. Put melody in her pauses (the music "breathes in her
  pauses"), or above E5 / below F3, or leave it to held notes that started before she spoke.
- **Light underneath**: at most 4 separate attacks a second while she speaks (`still`,
  `rolling` with `density=0.5`, held strings).
- The signature or a fragment, not the full tune, under a voice; the turn and the statement
  where she stops.

### 13. Very short films (under 20 s)

- **One idea, once.** A fragment of the cell, one turn, the logo. No full statement of the
  opening (the logo is the statement). At most 6 bars plus the ending.
- Subtle = fewer notes (`density=0.5`, `vary` with `"half"`/`"bass"`), `lead=0.85`,
  `shape="inward"`, `Piece(loudness=-17)`; still with a clear 3 dB shape and a peak.
- Make the effects for the film itself (see 15).

### 14. Making it subtle, interesting and varied: a checklist before rendering

Read the note dump (`render.sh --notes <film>`) and answer, bar by bar: where does the tune
rest? Which bar is the peak, and is its top note new there? What is new every 8 s? Does the
bass walk anywhere? Are any three bars the same? Is there a bar where only one instrument plays?
Is there one chord the listener has not heard before? If any answer is "nowhere" or "no", fix
the score before rendering.

### 15. Sound effects (made per film)

A film that needs effects defines `effects(music, plans, piece)` in its own file and returns
(audio, report rows): make each sound there (shaped noise, a filtered piano or string sample from
`engine/sounds.py`, or a recording made for this film), place it with `films.place_effect(out, x, t,
music, rel_db)` and soften pens with `films.soften(x, lp_hz)`. Pens: low-passed at 3.5-4.5 kHz,
rounded pen-down and lift-off, 8-12 dB under the music; check `above_5k_db` in the report. Never
read `../sfx-kit/` for a new film.

## The hard limits (checked on every render and by `render.sh --notes`)

A piece FAILS if it breaks any of these; the report says where in plain words. The older pieces
(the demos, "Own everything" and the first "Make it mine") are kept as the owner heard them and
fail several: that is the point of the checks.

| Check | Limit | Why this number |
|---|---|---|
| `bar-repeats` | a whole bar (tune, chords, bass, accompaniment, answer) heard at most 2 times | twice is statement and confirmation; a third identical bar is heard as a loop |
| `tune-bar-repeats` | any bar of the tune at most 2 times unchanged | the same, for the melody alone |
| `opening-statements` | the opening bar as written: 0 under 20 s, else 1 per 30 s (rounded) | the brand's strongest gesture; saved, it lands |
| `cell-same-form` | the cell in an identical form at most 3 times in any 30 s | identity yes, jingle no |
| `cell-present` | at least 1 before the logo (pieces over 8 s) | it must still sound like us |
| `nothing-new` | at most 8 s with no new tune bar, chord or accompaniment rhythm | two phrases at our usual speeds; in a cut-paced ad, attention renews every few seconds |
| `backing-static` | within any 6 bars, one accompaniment rhythm not heard in the 8 bars before | a groove may settle for a phrase and a half, then must move |
| `tune-breath` | at least 1 bar in every 8 with no tune note starting | a singer breathes; the answer needs the space |
| `chords-distinct` | at least min(7, 3 + seconds/15) different chords | one loop was half of what made the old film static |
| `chord-loop` | the chords may equal those 4 bars back for at most 4 bars running | one literal repeat of a progression, no more |
| `bass-moves` | in every 8 bars, at least one bass note that is not the chord's root | a bass that only plays roots marks time |
| `peak-named` / `peak-saved` | `Piece.peak` set (pieces over 12 s); the tune's highest note not heard before one bar ahead of it | the old film gave away its top note at 2.5 s |
| `answer-collisions` | no answer note starting with a tune note | call and response, not two people talking at once |
| `blend-pileup` | a blend bar at most 1.2 × the notes on either side | the old blend made the bars before the payoff the busiest |
| `tempo-steps` | no step over 2 % between beats (except after a held chord or a broadening) | the old ending lurched 4 % forward before the name |
| `tempo-fit` | every section within 6 % of its own `bpm` after fitting to the picture | further, a playful bounce turns frantic or a hush falls apart |
| `idle-layers` | no layer switched on that the pattern never plays (`pizz` under `bounce`) | a "strings join" that added nothing |
| `clashes` | no tune note on a borrowed chord's clashing note | the borrowed chords are colour, not a wrong note |
| `voice-room` | with `speech`: no melody note in F3-E5 starting while she speaks | her voice lives there |
| `voice-busy` | with `speech`: at most 4 attacks a second under her words | she must be easy to listen through |
| `loudness-range` (audio) | per-bar loudness range 4-14 dB (3-14 under 20 s) | the old film moved 2.8 dB in 54 s; over 14 is a jolt |
| `payoff-louder` (audio) | the peak's 2 bars at least 2 dB above the 4 bars before (2 under 15 s) | the old payoff arrived 1.7 dB *quieter* |
| `peak-loudest` (audio) | the loudest bar within the peak's neighbourhood, or the logo | the climax is where the picture says |
| `audio-nothing-new` (audio) | at most 12 s in which every bar's pitch content is a near copy (0.985) of an earlier bar | the same as `nothing-new`, measured on the sound itself |

The mix checks (loudness, true peak, clipping, mono, bands, every tune note on top at its pitch,
the logo landing, attacks) still apply to every piece, unchanged.

## Three worked plans

**A 10 s typographic film, "subtle" (stark turning bright, one pen).** 4 bars at about 96 + the
ending. Bar 1: `still`, the cell low and slow (`motif("signature").octave(-1).aug(2)`), lead 0.8,
over `Cm`. Bar 2: the tune rests; `vary={2: "bass"}`; the pen sound. Bar 3: the turn: `Ab` then
`Bb/D` (the bass rises), the cell answered a step higher on the second piano. Bar 4: `Ab/Bb`,
`density=0.5`, `shape="grow"`: the peak (`peak=` its downbeat). Ending: the logo on E-flat. Full
statement: none. Loudness range: about 4 dB.

**A 30 s passage under a narrator (resigned, then wonder).** 10-11 bars at about 76, `hopeful`
with `accomp="still"` then `"rolling"` at `density=0.5`. While she speaks: held strings, the
piano's chords, no melody between F3 and E5; in each of her pauses, a fragment of the cell
(`motif("signature").take(0, 2)`) in the piano, or the answer echoing it. Harmony one chord per
two bars, falling bass (`Eb`, `Eb/D`, `Cm7`, `Cm7/Bb`, `Abmaj7`...). At the turn word: a
`Db` → `Eb/G` (the "sun through clouds"), the strings open (`shape="swell"`), the tune's cell
rises in sequence above her range (`.octave(1)` or in her next pause). Peak at the turn word + 1
bar; then `shape="fade"`.

**A 60 s playful product film (three conversations).** 120 bpm, 28 bars. A (0-14 s): the bounce
at `density=0.6`, the cell alone twice, the second time `.up(2)`, the answer echoing in the rests.
B (14-30 s, the home): new pattern or `register=+12`, the first full phrase but *ending closed*,
`bass="walk"`, `vary={4: "thin", 8: "drop"}`. C (30-46 s, the money): contrast: the B phrase
(written), the tune in the cellos (`.octave(-1)` as `counter`), new chords (`Gm7`, `Ab/C`,
`Abm` once), `shape="arch"`; then `vary={8: "bass"}`: the drop. D (46-54 s, the payoff): the
full statement, at last in its top register, the violin and piano, the loudest bars, `Db` → `Eb`.
Ending: the logo, `land=56`, `ending_start` unpinned.

## How to work (the order)

1. Spot the picture; write the arc (section, seconds, loudness, register, density, tune guise).
2. Write the film file (`src/film_<key>.py`, below) with `peak=` and, if narrated, `speech=`.
3. `render.sh --notes <key>`: read the bar table (out/notes/<name>.txt) and the PASS/FAIL list.
   Fix the score until the symbolic checks pass. This takes seconds; do it many times.
4. `render.sh <key>`: renders and prints the mix checks and all composition checks, including
   the audio ones (out/films/<name>.checks.txt).
5. Report what the notes and the numbers show. Nobody here can listen: never say something
   "sounds" good.

## What is still weak (honest)

- **Nobody has listened to anything made under the new rules.** The checks prove a score is not
  a loop and has a shape; they cannot prove it is beautiful. The owner's ear decides.
- **The checks can be gamed** (a stray note to make a bar "new"). The note dump is there so a
  critic reads the score itself; the checks are the floor, not the goal.
- **The felt piano has only two recorded loudness layers**, so touch changes are part loudness,
  part tone; shapes beyond about ±2.5 dB on the piano alone come from the other instruments.
- **The answer that listens is automatic**; where it matters, write the answer by hand
  (`Section.answer=write(...)`).
- **Pinned ending times still lurch** if `ending_start` and `land` are both pinned; the check
  reports it; the fix is to leave `ending_start` unpinned.

## Technical appendix (for the composer)

**Commands** (from anywhere; about 40 s per film render, 1 s for notes):

```
scripts/campaign/sound/songbook/render.sh --notes mim       # notes bar by bar + composition checks, no audio
scripts/campaign/sound/songbook/render.sh mim               # render every film in src/film_mim.py, all checks
scripts/campaign/sound/songbook/render.sh make-it-mine      # one film by name; a demo by name
scripts/campaign/sound/songbook/render.sh                   # everything (demos and films)
```

Outputs (git-ignored): `out/films/<name>.wav` (and `<name>-no-pen.wav` when the film has
effects), `out/films/<name>.checks.txt` and `.json` (one per film, so composers working at the
same time never overwrite each other; `out/films/checks.txt` is rebuilt from them),
`out/notes/<name>.txt` (the bar table: bar, time, section, chords, tune notes, answer/counter
notes, bass, notes in the bar, accompaniment rhythm letter, NEW when something new happens;
after a render also each bar's loudness and its similarity to the closest earlier bar).

**A film in its own file**: `src/film_<key>.py` (key = short, lower case, e.g. `mim`). It is
found automatically; `render.sh <key>` renders every film it defines. It must expose `FILM`
(or `FILMS = [...]`). Film names must be new (do not reuse `make-it-mine`). Set `copy_to=None`
until the owner picks it.

```python
"""Make it mine, second score: <one line on the idea>."""
from films import Film, speech_from_words, place_effect, soften   # what you need
from motif import motif, write, rest
from score import Piece, Section as S

sig = motif("signature")
PIECE = Piece(
    "make-it-mine-r2", 60.0, land=56.0, peak=46.0, seed=41,        # style="v2" is the default
    notes="one line for the report",
    sections=[
        S("playful", 7, bpm=120, tune=[sig, rest(1), sig.up(2), rest(1), motif("phrase1").take(8, 16).bars(3)],
          density=0.6, vary={4: "thin"}, answer=None, label="the look"),
        S("playful", 8, bpm=120, start=14.0, tune=[...], chords=[["Eb/G"], ["Ab"], ...], bass="walk",
          counter=write("r/2 G3/2 | Ab3/4 | ..."), shape="arch", into="pickup", label="the home"),
        ...
    ])
FILM = Film("make-it-mine-r2", PIECE, picture=[(0.0, "the look"), (14.0, "the home")], copy_to=None)
```

**Section fields**: `mood`, `bars`, `tune` (a list of segment names `"full"`, `"phrase1"`,
`"phrase2"`, `"signature"`, `"rest"`, and/or `motif.Phrase` objects, laid end to end; bars past
the tune keep the accompaniment going), `start` (pin to a time), `bpm` (**give every section a
bpm**: a section without one gets its mood's usual speed, and a speed change between two sections
of the same mood is a jump), `voice` (`"piano"`, `"violin"`, `"both"`), `accomp` (`"rolling"`,
`"bounce"`, `"rocking"`, `"sparse"`, `"still"`), `add` / `drop` (layers `answer`, `pizz`,
`held`, `synth`, `sub`, `air`, `low`, `octaves`), `form`, `turn`, `lift`, `into` (`"auto"`,
`"blend"`, `"build"`, `"flow"`, `"pickup"`, `"drop"`, `"hold"`, `"breath"`), `span`, `hold`,
`narration`, `level_db`, `dyn`, `chords` (one list per bar, one or two chord names), `label`; and
the composing controls: `counter` + `counter_voice`, `answer` (a Phrase, or `False`), `bass`
(`"walk"`, `"pedal:Eb2"`, a Phrase), `vary` ({bar: `"thin"|"half"|"drop"|"bass"|"rest"|"full"`}),
`density` (0-1), `register` (semitones), `shape`, `broaden`, `push` (ms), `legato` (beats),
`lead`, `pedal`.

**Piece fields**: `name`, `length`, `sections`, `logo` (`"blend"` / `"piano"`), `land`,
`ending_start` (avoid), `rit` (default 0.85), `seed`, `loudness` (default -14 LUFS; -17 for a
subtle film), `peak` (seconds), `speech` ([(t0, t1)]), `style` (`"v2"`; `"v1"` only for the old
pieces), `notes`.

**Film fields**: `Film(name, piece, hits=[], picture=[...], copy_to=None, effects=None)`.
`effects(music, plans, piece)` returns `(audio array the length of music, report rows)`; use
`films.place_effect` for each sound (it returns the row) and `films.soften` for pens.
`films.speech_from_words(path, t_from, t_to, start_at=0.0)` turns a word-timing JSON into
`speech` spans.

**Phrases** (`src/motif.py`): `motif("bar1".."bar8" | "phrase1" | "phrase2" | "full" |
"signature" | "ending")`, `write("NOTE/BEATS ... | ...")` (`r` = rest, `@0.8` = softer; every bar
but the last must add up to 4 beats), `rest(n)`; methods `.up .down .octave .aug .dim .shift
.take .seq .invert .end_on .rhythm .fill .touch .bars`, `a + b` (in sequence), `a & b` (together).
Every note keeps its origin: plain tune notes are still checked note for note against the tune;
developed and hand-written notes are counted ("developed", "written") in the tune identity line.

**Timing to picture.** Every pinned time (each `start`, and `land`) splits the piece into spans;
inside a span every section's speed is scaled by the same factor so the span fills its time
exactly. The note dump prints each section's final speed and how far it moved from its usual
one: keep it within about 5 % (change a bar count, or add a `hold`). A blend into a different
speed spreads the change over its bars; the ending bar slows into the logo.

**Balance**: `mix.TARGET` (each instrument group's loudness while it plays: tune piano -19,
violin tune -17.5, counter-line -24.5, answer -24 ...), `mix.AIR_DB`, `score.SHINE`, the moods'
levels (`moods.py`), the lift (`blocks.LIFT`, `STRING_LIFT`), the shapes (`blocks.SHAPES`).
Narration: `level_db` per section (default -6 under `narration=True`) with a 3 dB dip at 2.5 kHz,
both changing over 3 seconds at section changes.

**Files**: `src/tune.py` (tune, answer, chords, voicings, `smooth_bass`) · `src/motif.py`
(phrases and development) · `src/moods.py` · `src/blocks.py` (blocks into notes; the composing
controls) · `src/score.py` (sections, timing, the players; `Band(dry=True)` logs notes without

`src/films.py` (the old films, `Film`, discovery of `film_<key>.py`) · `src/engine/` (signal
