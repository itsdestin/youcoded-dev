# The youcoded songbook

## What this is

Every advert, film and clip from now on gets its music from this one small set of building
blocks: **one tune, one set of chords, four ways of accompanying it, a handful of
instruments that can be switched on and off, one way to build up, and two endings with the
sound logo.** A new piece is made by listing sections ("four bars hopeful, then four bars
playful...") and the code plays it. Nobody writes new music for a new video, so all our music
is recognisably the same song family, the way a film's theme comes back in every scene.

The blocks come from the three pieces you said yes to: "One becomes many" (blend-1),
"Pizzicato bounce" (chamber-1) and "Rocking piano, singing strings" (chamber-2). Nothing comes
from the versions you said no to (both two-piano versions and "Mirror").

Listen to `out/`: four demonstrations, an extra, and the logo on its own (below).

## The tune

Key: E-flat major, like everything before. One bar is four beats.

| Bar | Notes | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **B-flat, G, F**, E-flat | the signature "you-co-ded", then one step further down |
| 2 | E-flat, high C, B-flat, G | the same shape, higher |
| 3 | A-flat, G, E-flat, C | falls back down |
| 4 | E-flat, then a long D | hangs open, like a question |
| 5-6 | the same as bars 1-2 | |
| 7 | high C, B-flat, A-flat, G | from the top |
| 8 | F, G, A-flat, B-flat | climbs back up toward home |
| ending | high C (long), B-flat (long) | a sigh onto B-flat, the logo's own first note |

The rhythm is mostly "long, long, longer, short": the short note pushes into the next bar,
which gives it a little skip.

**Why this tune.** It is the one chamber-1 and chamber-2 share, so two of the three pieces you
liked are already built on it, and it has what the rules ask for: a real tune that grows out
of the three signature notes instead of repeating them. blend-1's own tune was the three
notes said three times, each a step higher; you have said tunes should be more interesting than
that. What made blend-1 special was its *conversation* (a second piano answering every
phrase), so that became its own block, THE ANSWER, re-written to fit under this tune.

It can be used three ways: **in full** (8 bars), **just the first phrase** (bars 1-4, which
ends on a question) or **just the signature** (the three notes alone, then a bar of space:
for under a narrator).

## The blocks

**THE ANSWER** (from blend-1). After each bar of the tune, a second piano, on the other side,
answers with three notes climbing up a ladder into the next bar. The ladders climb higher as
the tune goes on; the second time round they are played in two-note harmony. It only answers
when the tune is playing, and it stops while the violin has the tune (one voice at a time).

**THE CHORDS.** One progression, in two forms:

- *Bright* (E-flat major): E-flat | C minor | A-flat | B-flat. The brand's chords.
- *Dark* (for a problem, a flat moment): C minor | A-flat | F minor | G. C minor is E-flat
  major's "relative minor": it uses exactly the same seven notes. So **the dark form changes
  not one note of the tune**; only the floor under it turns sad. The one new note, B natural,
  lives only in the accompaniment's G chord, which gives the minor key its pull.
- *The turn*: the dark form's last bar rises A-flat, B-flat, the classic way out of a minor
  key. Put it at the end of a dark section and the next bright section feels like the sun
  coming out, not like a change of record.

**THE ACCOMPANIMENT** (the steady engine under the tune; it never stops within a section and
works at any speed):

- **Rolling piano** (blend-1): the left hand rolls through each chord in even notes. Warm,
  flowing; pedalled in the warm moods.
- **Pizzicato bounce** (chamber-1): cellos pluck the low note, violas pluck little chords in
  between the beats, a soft piano chord underneath. Light and bouncy.
- **Rocking piano** (chamber-2): the left hand rocks low-high with a skip, cellos plucking the
  low note under it. Warm and lyrical.
- **Sparse** (new, for stark moments): one low open chord held under each chord, and a single
  note (G) pulsing slowly twice a bar, whatever the chord. Deadpan.
- (**Still**, for a hush: one soft rolled chord, touched again halfway. Barely moving.)

**THE LAYERS** (switched on or off per section):

- **Second-piano answers** (THE ANSWER, above).
- **Plucked strings**: cellos and violas plucking along with the accompaniment's rhythm.
- **Held strings**: three low bowed notes that move as little as possible; they never swell
  and dip, they only change level in long straight lines.
- **Solo violin takes the tune** (chamber-2): the violin sings it, the violin section doubles
  it quietly, the cellos sing it an octave lower. When the tune passes from piano to violin,
  the piano plays the first bar with the violin, softly, then lets go (you hear the hand-over).
- **Both**: the violin sings the tune while the piano plays it in octaves (the biggest).
- **Soft synth**: quietly doubles the accompaniment's moving line; its tone opens a little
  across a section (never a sweep).
- **Soft sub bass**: a round low note under each chord; weight, no rhythm. It fades in over a
  bar when it first arrives.
- **Low cellos**: hold each chord's lowest note (part of the lift; on in triumphant).
- **Air**: one high, quiet, held violin line (E-flat, moving to D only on the "leaning" chord).
  It is continuous, never an accent. It is there so the music is not dull on bright speakers.

**THE LIFT** (how a section grows into a big moment, using only the playing): across the
section everyone plays a little harder in one straight line (about 1.5 dB by its end), the held
strings rise further (about 3.5 dB), the cellos join low underneath and the high violins join
halfway (the sound gets wider at both ends), and the piano doubles the tune an octave lower.
The lift's level then stays up through the next section. No risers, no whooshes, no drums.

**TRANSITIONS** between sections (always musical):

- **Flow**: the next section simply starts on the next bar (levels meet over about a beat).
- **Build**: the last bar grows into the next section. If the next section is quicker or
  slower, the players speed up or slow down across that bar. If the section was sparse or
  still, the player starts the next section's pattern softly in its last bar (a "lead-in"), so
  the new section arrives already moving.
- **Hold**: the last chord is held for a beat or two (a pause on a chord), then the next section
  starts. Also the tool for landing on a cut that comes slightly late.
- **Breath**: like a hold, but nothing new is played; the last notes simply ring.

**THE ENDINGS**:

- **Into the full-blend logo** (any piece with music): a final bar of A-flat then B-flat; the
  tune sighs high C to B-flat; the band holds the chord and gives way as the logo starts
  (the logo's first note is that same B-flat). The music fades over 0.7 s around the logo's
  start; nothing new plays over the logo.
- **Into the piano-only logo**: the same bar, but the strings bow away and everything except the
  piano drops out first, so the piano-only logo is the same instrument carrying on.

## The moods

| Mood | Sounds like | Use it for | Usual speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stark** | the dark chords, the sparse pattern, the tune played plainly and dry, a low held string bed | a problem, a flat statement, an awkward silence | 66-100 beats a minute (80) |
| **Hush** | the piano almost still, far strings | the quiet moment between two feelings | 66-100 (80) |
| **Hopeful** | bright chords, rolling piano, held strings, air | under a narrator; a hopeful turn; slow builds | 72-96 (84) |
| **Playful** | pizzicato bounce, the answers, quick | product moments, things working | 108-128 (120) |
| **Triumphant** | rocking piano, violin and piano in octaves, everything on | the final line, the payoff, before the logo | 96-126 (112) |

Any section can add or drop single layers, swap the accompaniment, or set its own speed.

## The rules (your rulings, in your words where possible)

- **No clapping or clacking, ever**, no kick drum and no drum kit. Energy comes from
  instruments playing together. (Nothing drum-like even exists in the songbook's code.)
- **Never a fake-out or a "thwip"**: "it sounds like a 2005 browser demo switching pages". No
  stops answered by a flourish; no zips, sweeps, whooshes or risers.
- **One piece of music per film**: "we cant *just* have notes, we need consistent
  tunes/strings/chords/etc." One tune, one set of chords, a steady accompaniment, a few clear
  sections.
- **No scattered high jingles.** A high part may only be a continuous line (the air layer).
- **Volume must not bounce.** Under a narrator the music level is set by hand per section and
  changes only at section changes, slowly. No automatic dip on every word.
- **Music answers feelings; sound effects answer actions.** The music changes at a few story
  beats, through a musical transition; every click, type or swipe on screen belongs to the
  separate sound-effects kit, never to the music.
- **The voice always wins.**
- **Two logos**: "piano only if we're ever using the logo sound by itself without other music,
  but the full blend if at the end of an ad with our other music."

## How a film is scored with it

1. **Spot it.** Watch the cut and mark the handful of moments (usually 2-4) where the *feeling*
   changes: the problem appears, the turn, the payoff. Not every action: just the feelings.
2. **One section per feeling.** Pick a mood for each, and the tune segment it plays (the first
   phrase for a question, the full tune for a statement, just the signature under a voice).
3. **Pin the times.** Give each section the second it should start; give the logo the second
   the name should land. The code picks the exact speed so every section change lands on its
   cut (see the appendix). Choose bar counts that keep the speed close to the mood's usual one.
4. **Choose the transitions.** A build into a payoff, a hold into a cut that needs a beat of
   air, a lead-in out of a quiet passage.
5. **Narration.** Mark the sections where someone speaks: the music drops 6 dB there (or what you
   set) with a gentle dip in the speech range, changing slowly at the section changes only.
   Under a voice, play just the signature, not the full tune.
6. **Sound effects** go on their own layer, on the actions, after the music is set.

## The two logos

- `logo/10-full-blend.wav`: at the end of anything with our music (all four demonstrations).
- `logo/02-piano-only.wav`: when the logo plays alone, or after a piece that is piano only
  (see `out/example-piano-ending.wav`). `out/logo-alone.wav` is this file, unchanged.

In both, the name lands exactly 0.50 s after the file starts; the ending block places the file
so the name lands on the downbeat after the last bar. Both are pasted unchanged, 2 dB above
their own level (they are -16 LUFS alone; the pieces are -14), after all processing.

## What is still weak (honest)

- **Nobody has listened.** I set every balance by measurement. Expect level changes after a
  first listen; each is one number (appendix).
- **The air layer and the piano's "shine" are my judgement calls.** The piano recording has
  almost nothing above 8 kHz (measured: 85-90 dB below the note), so no tone control can
  brighten it. I added the string's own upper partials to the tune piano (pure tones that follow
  each note and die with it), a quiet high held violin line, and a strong treble lift in the
  master (+11 dB above 9 kHz). Together they bring every demo inside the brand's brightness range,
  two of them only just (31.2 and 31.5 against a limit of 32). If the tune sounds glassy or the
  high violin note is noticeable, turn them down: the music will then measure darker than the
  range (about 33-35).
- **The solo violin's quick notes only just stand on top** in the playful lift (weakest 1.6 dB):
  bowed notes start softly. A real violinist would dig in harder.
- **Stark sections move more** (6-7 dB in a second) because they are single notes over a quiet
  bed. That is the deadpan character, but it is the least steady material.
- **The logo is still eighth notes at 120 beats a minute**, so after a slow piece (83 or 100) it
  is a small change of gear; the held chord before it softens this.
- **The sparse pattern's pulse is one note repeated**, and the piano has only two recorded
  loudness layers; I vary each note's tone slightly, but it may still read as mechanical.
- **Speed changes are smooth but quick** (the hopeful-to-playful speed-up happens over one bar).
- **The plucked strings are recordings of a section**, so each pluck is a few players slightly
  apart: warm, a little less crisp than one player.

## Technical appendix (for the next composer)

**Render everything:** `scripts/campaign/sound/songbook/render.sh` (about 40 s; one piece:
`render.sh demo-journey`). It needs the recordings the neighbouring folders download
(SOURCES.md) and their Python environment (or `songbook/.venv` from `requirements.txt`).

**Files:** `src/tune.py` (tune, answer, chords, voicings) · `src/moods.py` (presets) ·
`src/blocks.py` (everything that turns blocks into notes) · `src/score.py` (sections, timing,
the players) · `src/mix.py` (balance, narration levels, mastering) · `src/measure.py` and
`src/build.py` (render and check) · `src/demos.py` (the demonstrations: the worked examples) ·
`src/engine/` (signal processing and the instruments; nothing in it can make a drum).

**Describe a piece** (add it to `DEMOS` in `src/demos.py`, or build a list elsewhere and call
`build.one(piece)`):

```python
Piece("my-film", 50.0, logo="blend", sections=[
    S("stark", 3, tune="phrase1", turn=True, into="hold", hold=2.0, label="the problem"),
    S("hopeful", 4, tune=["signature", "signature"], start=10.0, narration=True, into="build"),
    S("playful", 8, tune=["phrase1", "phrase2"], start=21.5, add=("held",)),
    S("triumphant", 4, tune="phrase2", start=37.5, bpm=120, lift=True),
])   # the name lands at length - 2.5 s (full-blend) or - 2.0 s (piano); or set land=
# -> stark 84, hopeful 79.5 (speeding up to 120 in its last bar), playful 120, triumphant 120
```

Section fields: `mood`, `bars`, `tune` (`"full"`, `"phrase1"`, `"phrase2"`, `"signature"`,
`"rest"` or a list of them; bars beyond the tune keep the accompaniment going), `start`
(seconds; pins the section to the picture), `bpm` (its usual speed), `voice` (`"piano"`,
`"violin"`, `"both"`), `accomp` (`"rolling"`, `"bounce"`, `"rocking"`, `"sparse"`, `"still"`),
`add` / `drop` (layers: `answer`, `pizz`, `held`, `synth`, `sub`, `air`, `low`, `octaves`),
`form` (`"bright"` / `"dark"`), `turn`, `lift`, `into` (`"flow"`, `"build"`, `"hold"`,
`"breath"`), `hold` (beats), `narration`, `level_db`, `dyn`, `chords` (explicit, rarely).

**Timing to picture.** Every pinned time (each `start`, and the logo landing) splits the piece
into spans; inside a span every section's speed is scaled by the same factor so the span fills
its time exactly (to the sample). The report prints each section's final speed and how far it
moved from its usual one: keep it within about 5 %. If it moves more, change a bar count (one
bar more or less), or add a `hold` of a beat or two at the section's end. A `build` into a
different speed puts a smooth speed-up or slow-down in the last bar (`score.TempoMap`).

**Balance** is stated as numbers: each instrument group's loudness while it plays
(`mix.TARGET`, in LUFS; the tune piano -19, the violin tune -17.5, the air line -27...), the
master treble lift (`mix.AIR_DB`), the piano shine (`score.SHINE`), the moods' levels
(`moods.py`), the lift's size (`blocks.LIFT`, `STRING_LIFT`). Narration: `level_db` per
section (default -6 under `narration=True`), with a 3 dB dip at 2.5 kHz; both change over
3 seconds centred on the section change.

**Checks** run on every render (`out/checks.txt`): loudness, true peak, clipping, clean ends,
mono, low end, air, every tune note on top at its pitch (by rendering the piece again without
the tune), every tune note equal to the songbook's tune, every attack belonging to a played
note, attack noisiness, steadiness per section, logo identity.
